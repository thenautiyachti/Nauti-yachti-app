# Creator Management Agreement — working draft

> **This is a draft for a lawyer to review, not a document to sign.** It is
> written to be clear and fair rather than maximally favourable, for a specific
> reason: an agreement a creator did not understand is an agreement she can
> later say she was misled into, and this business depends on people trusting it
> enough to hand over their income.
>
> Every `[BRACKETED]` item is a decision nobody has made yet.
>
> Have an attorney licensed in `[STATE]` review this before it goes to anyone.
> The clauses on account access, content licensing and termination are the ones
> that decide what happens when things go wrong, and those are worth an hour of
> professional time.

## 1. What the Agency does

The Agency provides management services for the Creator's subscription-platform
accounts, being:

a. day-to-day operation of the Creator's accounts on `[PLATFORMS]`;
b. marketing, promotion and audience growth;
c. messaging with subscribers on the Creator's accounts (see clause 4);
d. scheduling and publication of content the Creator supplies;
e. pricing, sales and subscriber retention;
f. collection of earnings and payment of the Creator's share.

The Agency does not produce content, does not direct the content the Creator
creates beyond the general expectations in clause 3, and does not require the
Creator to produce any specific act, scene or material.

## 2. What the Creator owns

**The Creator owns all content she produces.** Nothing in this agreement
transfers ownership of any photograph, video, audio or other material.

The Creator grants the Agency a non-exclusive, revocable licence to publish,
promote, price and distribute that content **on her own accounts only**, for the
term of this agreement and for no other purpose. The licence ends when this
agreement ends (see clause 10).

The Agency may not: sell, licence or distribute the Creator's content anywhere
other than her own accounts; use it in advertising outside those accounts
without her written permission each time; or retain or use it after termination
except as clause 10 requires.

## 3. What the Creator provides

The Creator agrees to supply:

- `[N]` pieces of content per `[week / month]`, of the types agreed in writing;
- content she owns outright, featuring only herself and any person who has
  signed a release and provided age verification;
- content that complies with the terms of service of every platform it will
  appear on.

Expectations will be set in advance and in writing. The Agency will tell the
Creator what it needs and when; the Creator decides what she is willing to make.

**The Creator may refuse any request for any reason, at any time.** Refusing a
specific request is not a breach of this agreement.

## 4. Account access and who messages subscribers

**This clause exists to make sure nothing here is a surprise later. Read it.**

a. The Agency and its personnel — which includes employees, contractors,
   chatters and virtual assistants — **will have login access to the Creator's
   accounts**.

b. **Agency personnel will send messages to subscribers from the Creator's
   account, in the Creator's voice, and subscribers will generally believe they
   are speaking with the Creator.** The Creator acknowledges she understands and
   consents to this.

c. The Agency will keep a record of who has access to the Creator's account and
   will provide it on request. Access is removed when a person stops working on
   her accounts.

d. Agency personnel are bound by confidentiality and may not disclose,
   distribute or retain the Creator's private communications, images or personal
   information.

e. The Creator may at any time require that messaging be limited or stopped
   entirely, on `[N]` days' notice. `[NOTE: this may change the split — decide
   and state it here.]`

f. The Agency will not, from the Creator's account: solicit or arrange any
   in-person meeting or sexual service; misrepresent the Creator's location or
   personal circumstances in a way intended to obtain money; or impersonate the
   Creator outside the platform.

> **Why this clause is written so plainly:** third-party messaging is the
> subject of active litigation in which management agencies are named
> defendants. Informed, recorded, specific consent from the Creator is both the
> honest thing and the thing that matters if anyone ever asks.

## 5. The split

| | |
|---|---|
| Platform fee | Taken by the platform first (OnlyFans: 20%) |
| Creator's share | `[X]%` of **net** earnings (after the platform's fee) |
| Agency's share | `[Y]%` of net earnings |

`[DECIDE: is the split on NET (after the platform's cut) or GROSS (before it)?`
`This must be stated explicitly. A "50/50 deal" written on gross pays the`
`Creator 62.5% of what actually arrives. Ambiguity here is the single most`
`common dispute in this industry.]`

The Agency pays all costs of its own operations — staff, advertising, software
and processing — out of its share. **No agency cost is deducted from the
Creator's share** unless she has agreed to it in writing in advance.

## 6. Payment

- Paid `[monthly / twice monthly]`, within `[N]` days of the end of each period.
- By `[method]`, in `[USD]`.
- With a statement showing gross earnings, the platform fee, the split applied
  and the amount paid.
- Minimum payout `$[N]`; anything below it carries to the next period and is
  never forfeited.

The Creator may request the underlying earnings records at any time and the
Agency will provide them within `[N]` days.

**Refunds and chargebacks:** where a platform reverses earnings already paid
out, the reversed amount is deducted from the Creator's next payment. The Agency
will show the reversal on the statement.

## 7. Age, identity and records

The Creator confirms she is **18 years of age or older** and will provide a
government-issued photo ID before any content is published.

The Agency is required by 18 U.S.C. § 2257 to keep records of the legal name and
date of birth of every performer appearing in content it publishes, and to
produce them on lawful inspection. The Creator agrees to provide these records
and to keep them current.

Identity documents are held in secure storage, accessible only to personnel who
need them, and are never stored alongside operational business records. They are
retained for the period the law requires and destroyed afterwards.

**Any other person appearing in the Creator's content** must provide the same
records and a signed release before that content is published. No exceptions.

## 8. Independent contractor

The Creator is an independent contractor, not an employee. She controls what
content she makes, when and how. She is responsible for her own taxes, and will
provide a completed `[W-9 / W-8BEN]` before the first payment.

Nothing in this agreement prevents the Creator from working elsewhere, unless
clause 9 says otherwise.

## 9. Exclusivity

`[DECIDE — pick one and delete the other:]`

**Option A (non-exclusive):** The Creator may work with other agencies or manage
other accounts herself. The Agency manages only the accounts listed in
Schedule 1.

**Option B (exclusive):** For the term, the Creator will not engage another
agency to manage the accounts in Schedule 1. She remains free to work in any
other field and to end this agreement under clause 10.

> Recommended: **Option A for the first creators.** Exclusivity is something an
> agency earns by producing results, and demanding it before you have a track
> record is how you lose the people worth signing.

## 10. Ending this agreement

**Either party may end this agreement on `[30]` days' written notice, for any
reason or no reason.** Neither party owes the other a fee for doing so.

Either party may end it immediately if the other materially breaches it and does
not fix the breach within `[14]` days of being told.

When this agreement ends:

a. **The accounts belong to the Creator.** The Agency will transfer all logins,
   passwords, two-factor methods and recovery details to her within `[7]` days,
   and will remove all Agency access.
b. The Agency's licence to her content ends. It will delete every copy of her
   content in its possession within `[30]` days, except records § 2257 requires
   it to keep.
c. The Agency will pay everything owed to the Creator within `[30]` days, with a
   final statement.
d. The Agency will provide a list of subscribers and any content queue, so she
   can continue without interruption.
e. **The Agency has no continuing claim on the Creator's future earnings.**

> **On that last line:** some agencies write in a tail — a share of earnings for
> months after the creator leaves. Do not. It is the term that makes people
> refuse to sign, it is the term that gets challenged, and having just read a
> perpetual royalty clause from the other side of the table, we know exactly how
> it feels.

## 11. Confidentiality and privacy

Each party will keep the other's confidential information private, during and
after the term.

The Agency will protect the Creator's personal information, restrict access to
personnel who need it, and will not sell or share it. The Agency will tell the
Creator without delay if her personal information is exposed in a breach.

The Creator's legal name, address and identity documents will not be disclosed
to subscribers or any third party except where the law requires it.

## 12. General

- **Independent legal advice.** The Creator has been given the opportunity to
  have this agreement reviewed by her own lawyer before signing and has been
  encouraged to do so.
- **Entire agreement.** This document is the whole agreement. Changes must be in
  writing and signed by both parties.
- **Governing law.** `[STATE]`, and the courts of `[COUNTY, STATE]`.
- **Severability.** If a clause is unenforceable, the rest stands.
- **Assignment.** Neither party may assign this agreement without the other's
  written consent.

## Notes for us, not for the agreement

Delete this section before sending.

**Decisions still needed:**

1. The split, and whether it is on net or gross (clause 5)
2. Content volume expectations (clause 3)
3. Exclusive or non-exclusive (clause 9)
4. Payment frequency, method and minimum (clause 6)
5. The legal entity and state (clause 12)
6. Notice period (clause 10)

**Why several clauses favour the creator:**

Clauses 2, 4(e), 10(a), 10(e) and 12 all give the Creator more than the industry
minimum. That is deliberate and it is not charity:

- These are the terms people check with a lawyer or a friend. Being clean on
  them is what makes a good creator sign with two unknown guys over an
  established agency.
- Every one of them reduces the chance of a dispute where the Creator says she
  did not understand what she agreed to — which is the claim that actually
  threatens this business.
- A creator who can leave freely and chooses to stay is worth more than one who
  is locked in and resentful. The locked-in one stops producing, and there is no
  clause that fixes that.

**Clause 4 is the one to not water down.** Explicit, recorded consent to
third-party messaging is the difference between a documented commercial
arrangement and the allegation being litigated against other agencies right now.

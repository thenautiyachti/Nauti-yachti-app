## 1. What this is

You make the content. We run the business side of your accounts. We share what
they earn, on the terms set out in clause 5.

You are an independent contractor, not an employee. You control what you create,
when, and how. You are responsible for your own taxes.

## 2. What we do

Velvet Angels provides:

- Marketing and advertising strategy
- Social media promotion
- Subscriber messaging and engagement *(read clause 4 — this one matters)*
- Content ideas, planning and direction
- Promotional strategy and campaign planning
- Audience growth
- Day-to-day account management
- Sales and upselling strategy
- Performance tracking and reporting
- Ongoing business guidance

We do not produce content, and we do not require you to create any specific act,
scene or material.

## 3. What you do

- Create and upload content on the schedule we agree
- Follow the content strategy we develop **together**
- Provide promotional material when we ask
- Keep us posted on upcoming content and campaigns
- Provide a government photo ID before anything is published (clause 8)

Volume and type are agreed in writing at the start and can be renegotiated at
any time: `[N]` pieces per `[week/month]`, of the types listed in Schedule 1.

**You may refuse any request, for any reason, at any time.** Refusing a specific
request is not a breach of this agreement. If refusals make the arrangement
unworkable, we talk about it — we do not penalise you for it.

## 4. Account access, and who messages your subscribers

**Read this clause properly. Ask us about anything in it.**

a. Velvet Angels personnel — employees, contractors, chatters and virtual
   assistants — **will have login access to your accounts.**

b. **Our team will message your subscribers from your account, in your voice,
   and those subscribers will generally believe they are talking to you.** You
   confirm you understand this and agree to it.

c. We keep a record of everyone with access to your account and will give it to
   you whenever you ask. Access is removed when a person stops working on your
   accounts.

d. Everyone with access is bound by confidentiality. They may not disclose,
   distribute or keep your private messages, images or personal information.

e. **You can tell us to limit or stop the messaging at any time**, on `[14]`
   days' notice. It will change what we can do for you and we would want to
   revisit the split — but it is your account and your call.

f. From your account, we will never: arrange any in-person meeting or service;
   misrepresent where you are or what is happening in your life in order to get
   money out of someone; or contact anyone as you outside the platform.

> This is spelled out because informed consent, given once, in writing, is the
> only version of third-party messaging that is fair to you — and because
> agencies that left it unsaid are currently defendants in litigation over it.

## 5. The money

### 5.1 How it divides

**50% Creator / 50% Velvet Angels**, applied to **net platform earnings after
approved Marketing Costs**.

In order:

1. The platform takes its own fee first (OnlyFans: 20%). Nobody here controls that.
2. **Approved Marketing Costs** (clause 5.2) come off what is left.
3. What remains — the **Divisible Amount** — splits 50/50.

### 5.2 Marketing Costs — the part to read twice

"Marketing Costs" means advertising and promotional spend **that you approved in
writing, in advance.**

- Nothing is deducted unless you approved it first.
- Approved spend is capped at **`$[N]` per month**, or `[N]%` of that month's net
  earnings, whichever is lower — unless you agree in writing to more.
- Every statement itemises exactly what was spent and on what. Receipts on request.
- Anything we spend that you did not approve comes out of **our** half, not the
  Divisible Amount.

**All our other costs are ours.** Staff, chatters, software, payment processing,
our own time — none of it touches your share.

### 5.3 A worked example

On $10,000 of gross subscriber payments in a month, with $800 of marketing you
approved:

| | |
|---|---:|
| Subscribers pay | $10,000 |
| Platform fee (20%) | −$2,000 |
| **Reaches the account** | **$8,000** |
| Approved Marketing Costs | −$800 |
| **Divisible Amount** | **$7,200** |
| **Your 50%** | **$3,600** |
| Velvet Angels' 50% | $3,600 |

If you approved no marketing that month, the Divisible Amount is $8,000 and you
receive $4,000.

## 8. Age, identity and legal records

You confirm you are **18 or older** and will provide a government photo ID before
anything is published.

Federal law (18 U.S.C. § 2257) requires the producer of adult content to hold the
legal name and date of birth of every performer appearing in it, and to produce
those records on lawful inspection. Velvet Angels keeps them.

Your documents are held in secure storage, reachable only by people who need
them, never stored alongside ordinary business records, and never shown to a
subscriber. They are kept for the period the law requires and destroyed
afterwards. **Your legal name and address are never disclosed to subscribers.**

**Anyone else appearing in your content** must provide the same records and a
signed release before that content is published. No exceptions, and no content
featuring another person goes out without it.

## 9. Taxes

You provide a completed `[W-9 / W-8BEN]` before your first payment. We issue the

withholding anything.

## 10. Confidentiality and your privacy

Each of us keeps the other's confidential information private, during and after.

We will protect your personal information, limit access to people who need it,
and never sell or share it. If your personal information is ever exposed in a
breach, we tell you without delay.

## 11. What we don't promise

**We cannot and do not guarantee any level of earnings.** What you make depends
on your content, your consistency, your audience and conditions on the platform
— much of which neither of us controls.

Any figure discussed in a consultation, or mentioned anywhere, is an illustration
and not a promise. Earnings on these platforms vary enormously and most accounts
earn modestly. **We will give you our honest view of what we think is
achievable. It is an opinion, not a commitment, and nothing in this agreement
should be read as one.**

## 12. Ending this agreement

**Either of us may end it on `[30]` days' written notice, for any reason or no
reason. No fee, no penalty.**

Either of us may end it immediately if the other seriously breaches it and does
not fix that within `[14]` days of being told.

When it ends:

a. **Your accounts are yours.** Within `[7]` days we hand over every login,
   password, two-factor method and recovery detail, and remove our own access.
b. Our licence to your content ends. We delete every copy we hold within `[30]`
   days, apart from the § 2257 records we are legally required to keep.
c. Everything owed to you is paid within `[30]` days, with a final statement.
d. We give you your subscriber list and any content queue, so nothing you built
   is lost.
e. **We take no share of anything you earn afterwards.** No tail, no residual,
   no continuing claim.

### Why clause 5.2 is written so tightly

"50/50 after agreed-upon advertising and marketing expenses" is the single
riskiest line in the concept, because it is the one that can quietly reduce a
creator's share to nothing. *"We spent $6,000 on ads this month, so there is
$2,000 to split"* is a conversation that ends the relationship and invites a
claim.

Three things prevent it: **she approves it in advance in writing**, there is a
**hard cap**, and **unapproved spend comes out of our half**. All three, or the
clause is an open tap on her income.

Note also what it means commercially — marketing comes off the top, so **we are
spending fifty-cent dollars.** Every dollar of approved ad spend costs us fifty
cents and costs her fifty cents. That is fair only while she genuinely approves
it, which is why the approval has to be real and not a box in an onboarding form.

### Why clause 11 exists

It is the counterweight to any earnings figure said out loud in a consultation.
Unsubstantiated earnings claims are an FTC Section 5 problem and, more
immediately, exactly what an unhappy creator points at later. The clause does not
make a spoken promise safe — **it is not a licence to promise six figures on a
call** — it just makes sure the written agreement never contradicts the honest
version.

### The internal 55/45

David is right that the partnership split does not belong in this document — it
is a separate agreement between the two partners and no business of any creator.

Worth knowing: it does not shield either partner. If a creator ever brings a
claim, both partners and the entity are in it regardless of how the internal
share is divided.

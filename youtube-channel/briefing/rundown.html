CAKE PANCAKE

How this will work

One afternoon a week. That's the whole commitment.

Every week

1
He records one session Tablet screen recorder, landscape, mic ON. He picks one daft rule before he starts — blindfolded, or nobody can talk. That rule is the video.
2
I cut it down Software finds the loudest, busiest moments so nobody sits through two hours of footage. One longer video plus three short clips, from the same recording.
3
A parent watches it — all of it Before anything is public. Mostly catching the accidental: a friend using his real name, something in the background.
4
It goes up YouTube, then the same short clips on TikTok and Reels. Four posts a week from one afternoon — that's the trick, not working four times as hard.

The rules we're holding

  • No face on camera. His Roblox character is the character.
  • Cake Pancake, not his real name. No school, no town, no street.
  • Friends' parents get asked first — their kid's voice, their call.
  • Nothing posts unwatched. No exceptions, including the short clips.
  • Comments are off. YouTube does that automatically for kids' content — it's the bit that makes me comfortable with this at all.

Who does what

None of it lands on one person.

Him THE FUN BIT

  • Plays, talks, picks the rule
  • Says what the video should be called
  • Decides which clips he likes

You WHENEVER

  • Be in some — you losing at Roblox is genuinely the best video on there
  • Record at yours too, so it's not only my weekends
  • Second pair of eyes before things go up
  • The friends' parents, if you know them better than I do

Me THE BORING BIT

  • Cutting, uploading, thumbnails
  • Keeping the account and the settings right
  • Making sure it stays one afternoon a week
What to expect: almost certainly not much, and that's fine — most channels stay small. What he actually gets out of it is learning to make something and finish it.

Let's look at it properly in three months and be honest about whether it's still fun. Stopping is a completely fine outcome.
